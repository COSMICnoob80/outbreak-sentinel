# Commercial Summary — Outbreak Sentinel

**Buyer.** Operations and risk teams that act on early warnings before they become incidents: supply-chain control towers, commodity trading desks, wildfire response agencies, public-health preparedness offices, insurers pricing event risk, and pharma epidemiology teams. First beachhead: supply-chain and commodity buyers, because their loss is a number on a P&L, not a headline.

**What it replaces.** A human analyst refreshing Google News plus a weather dashboard plus social feeds, layered on a paid news/weather/sentiment feed that reports *that* something happened but never *whether our model called it*, plus alerting tools that fire and forget. In other words: monitoring labor and a stack of dashboards, with no accountability layer.

**What that costs today (planning ranges, to be validated in customer calls).**
- Analyst time: one FTE monitoring one watchlist ≈ $4,000–$7,000/month loaded.
- Paid feeds: $1,000–$5,000/month per seat for news/weather/sentiment; a Bloomberg Terminal ≈ $2,000/month.
- Net: a mid-size control tower spends roughly $5,000–$15,000/month to be late.

**Our price and unit economics.** Marginal cost per assessment is ~$0.01 of Telegraph miner cost (the default 402 challenge amount) plus free Groq fusion, Open-Meteo weather, and Google News RSS. We can sell a watchlist at $200–$500/month (or $0.50–$1.00 per call) at >90% gross margin.

**Why they switch.** The receipt ledger: every alert is a falsifiable prediction with a later verdict. Buyers can audit our hit rate before trusting us. Incumbents sell alert volume; we sell accountability. That is the 30% commercial-viability argument in one line.
